SOAP Note Individual Session · Substance Use Disorder · Session #4
S — Subjective
Client J.M. (32, M) presented for scheduled individual session appearing alert and cooperative. Client reported feeling "better than last week" but noted ongoing sleep disturbances, stating: "I wake up around 3am and my mind just starts racing about everything I've done." Denied active cravings for alcohol in the past 72 hours. Reports attending one AA meeting since last session. Support from spouse described as "improving." No reports of self-harm ideation or suicidal thoughts.
O — Objective
Client arrived 5 minutes early. Appearance appropriate — well groomed, dressed casually. Affect was euthymic with moments of dysphoria when discussing past behaviours. Speech was clear, coherent, and goal-directed. No observable signs of intoxication or withdrawal. Motor activity within normal limits. PHQ-9 completed at session start: score 9 (mild depression), down from 13 at intake. Client engaged actively in discussion for full 50-minute session.
A — Assessment
Client is demonstrating early stabilisation consistent with early recovery from Alcohol Use Disorder (AUD), Moderate (F10.20). Sleep disturbance and ruminative thinking are consistent with post-acute withdrawal syndrome (PAWS) and unresolved guilt. Insight into substance use triggers is developing — client correctly identified social isolation as a precursor to past use. Motivation for change appears intrinsic and is strengthening. Risk for relapse remains moderate given early stage of recovery; protective factors include active 12-step engagement and spousal support.
P — Plan
1. Continue weekly individual sessions; next session scheduled in 7 days.
2. Introduce sleep hygiene psychoeducation handout — review at next session.
3. Assign CBT thought log to document nighttime rumination patterns.
4. Encourage attendance at minimum 2 AA meetings before next session.
5. Discuss guilt and shame processing in next session using cognitive restructuring framework.
6. Consult with prescribing physician re: sleep disturbance if no improvement by session #6.
Counselor: Levis, RAC  |  Date: [Session Date]  |  Duration: 50 min  |  Modality: Individual CBT
DAP Note Group Session · Trauma & Recovery · Session #7
D — Data
Client A.W. (27, F) attended scheduled group therapy session (8 participants). Session topic: Identifying Emotional Triggers in Recovery. Client arrived on time, chose a seat in the middle of the group circle — a notable change from previous sessions where she consistently sat near the exit. Client was initially quiet for the first 15 minutes. When invited to share, client disclosed for the first time that her substance use was closely linked to a period of domestic violence, stating: "I didn't realise the drinking was how I was coping with what happened to me at home." Response from group was supportive and empathetic. Client became tearful but regulated within approximately 3 minutes, using deep breathing without prompting. She declined individual check-in after the session but stated she felt "relieved" by the disclosure.
A — Assessment
Client demonstrated significant therapeutic progress in this session. First voluntary disclosure of trauma-substance use link suggests growing psychological safety within the group and increasing readiness for deeper trauma-focused work. Self-regulation during emotional distress (spontaneous use of breathing technique) indicates skill generalisation from individual sessions. Seating choice shift is a behavioural marker of reduced hypervigilance. Diagnoses remain: AUD, Mild (F10.10) and PTSD (F43.10). Trauma processing readiness is emerging; client may be appropriate for trauma-focused CBT or EMDR referral within 2–4 weeks pending further assessment.
P — Plan
1. Schedule individual session within 48 hours to follow up on today's disclosure in a private setting.
2. Provide written psychoeducation resource on trauma-substance use connection (van der Kolk framework).
3. Raise trauma-focused treatment referral in next multidisciplinary team meeting.
4. Continue group attendance — monitor participation and comfort levels.
5. Document disclosure in client's treatment file with appropriate confidentiality notation.
Counselor: Levis, RAC  |  Date: [Session Date]  |  Duration: 90 min  |  Modality: Group Therapy
Treatment Plan Initial Plan · Cannabis Use Disorder · Inpatient Setting
Client Profile & Diagnosis
Client: K.M. (19, M)  |  Admission Date: [Date]  |  Level of Care: Inpatient Rehabilitation

Primary Diagnosis: Cannabis Use Disorder, Severe (F12.20)
Secondary: Major Depressive Disorder, Moderate (F32.1)
Presenting Concerns: Daily cannabis use (5–8 joints/day) over 3 years; academic failure; social withdrawal; passive suicidal ideation (no plan or intent); family conflict.
Goal 1 — Achieve & Maintain Abstinence
Objective 1.1: Client will identify and verbalise a minimum of 3 personal triggers for cannabis use within 2 weeks of admission.
Objective 1.2: Client will develop a written relapse prevention plan with identified coping strategies by Week 3.
Intervention: Individual CBT sessions (2×/week); psychoeducation on addiction neuroscience; trigger mapping exercise.
Target Date: End of Week 3  |  Responsible: Primary Counselor
Goal 2 — Address Depressive Symptoms
Objective 2.1: Client will report a reduction in PHQ-9 score from baseline (16) to below 10 within 4 weeks.
Objective 2.2: Client will engage in one structured activity per day (exercise, journaling, or creative task) as monitored by daily log.
Intervention: Behavioural activation plan; referral to psychiatrist for medication evaluation; weekly mood tracking.
Target Date: End of Week 4  |  Responsible: Counselor + Psychiatry
Goal 3 — Rebuild Family Communication
Objective 3.1: Client and primary caregiver will attend a minimum of 2 family therapy sessions before discharge.
Objective 3.2: Client will demonstrate one new communication skill (active listening or "I statements") in a supervised family session.
Intervention: Family therapy (structured Bowenian approach); psychoeducation for family on addiction as a disorder.
Target Date: Week 5  |  Responsible: Family Therapist + Primary Counselor
Discharge & Aftercare Planning
Target discharge: 6 weeks from admission, contingent on sustained abstinence and PHQ-9 <10. Aftercare plan to include: outpatient counseling (1×/week), continued AA/NA attendance, academic re-engagement plan, 90-day check-in with primary counselor.

About Me

I've Sat on Both Sides of the Clinical Table.

My name is Levis. I'm a Resident Addiction Counselor with over 4 years at Lifetime Wellness Center, an inpatient rehabilitation facility in Nairobi, Kenya. I know what it means to write SOAP notes at midnight, manage a complex caseload, coordinate multidisciplinary care — and still show up fully present in session.

That background is why I'm not just another virtual assistant. I understand the documentation, the clinical language, the stakes of confidentiality, and the weight of getting it right. When I draft a note or organise a client file, I do it with the same care I applied to my own clients.

I'm also the author of Clinical Documentation Made Simple — a practical guide on SOAP & DAP notes, treatment planning, and diagnosis recording, written for counseling students and practitioners across Kenya and internationally.
